## 1. Strategic Positioning

**Locked category:** Energy solutions company. Power systems company.

**Locked position:**

> Takeshi Energy is a dependable energy systems company that designs, installs and supports power solutions around how people and properties actually operate.

**Not this:**

| Rejected identity | Why |
|-------------------|-----|
| Solar company | Solar is a technology, not the whole business |
| Solar-panel retailer | Implies catalogue-first selling |
| Equipment reseller | Implies brand names over system design |
| Generic electrical contractor | Understates assessment, storage, hybrid and support |
| Renewable-energy lifestyle brand | Greenwash / generic category language |

**Category hierarchy (locked):**

```text
ENERGY SOLUTIONS
        ↓
POWER SYSTEMS
        ↓
Solar + Inverter + Battery + Backup + Electrical infrastructure
```

If Takeshi later adds larger commercial systems, estate infrastructure, maintenance programmes, or other energy technologies, this hierarchy still holds. The site does not need to be rebuilt around “solar.”

**Operating model (supplied, not invented):**

Conversation → technical assessment where required → recommendation → procurement → installation → testing/commissioning → handover → support.

**Capability (supplied):** more than ten years of hands-on industry experience covering load calculation, system sizing, installation, electrical wiring, component matching, troubleshooting, commissioning and after-sales support.

**Product experience (supplied, supporting only):** Deye, Growatt, Felicity, Choice, Sukhig, and other suitable certified products selected by project requirement and availability.

## 4. Audience Definition

The site speaks to people **responsible for a property or operation’s power requirements**.

### Primary groups

| Group | Who | Technical level |
|-------|-----|-----------------|
| Residential | Homeowners; residential property managers | Mixed; often non-specialist |
| Commercial | Business owners; facilities / operations managers | Mixed; may be technically literate |
| Property / estate | Developers; estate managers; multi-unit decision-makers | Project / infrastructure literate |
| Institutional / specialized | Healthcare, fuel stations, hospitality, industrial | Continuity and critical-load literate |

Other properties affected by unreliable electricity are in scope. The site should not pretend to be only residential or only industrial.

### Dual-readership rule (locked)

> Technical enough for the engineer. Simple enough for the property owner.

Do not solve this by becoming vague. Solve it with **clear structure + plain language + useful technical specificity**.

---

## 5. Audience Needs / Jobs

Jobs to be done (what a visitor is trying to accomplish):

| Audience | Jobs | What the page must make clear |
|----------|------|-------------------------------|
| Residential | Keep essential loads running; protect equipment; understand cost and backup time; know how to start | Practical sizing, backup duration, maintenance, plain explanation |
| Commercial | Reduce downtime; match load and operating hours; plan expansion; make cost more predictable | Continuity, performance, maintenance, system design |
| Property / estate | Coordinate multiple units; integrate during construction; standardise options; plan maintenance | Scale, phasing, infrastructure, coordination |
| Institutional / specialized | Protect critical loads (clinical, pumps, refrigeration, payment, guest experience, production) | Critical-load thinking, electrical integration, support |

**Shared job:** “Tell someone who understands power systems what this property actually needs to do.”

The site is not trying to close a same-day retail purchase. It is trying to start a competent conversation.

---

## 6. Brand Personality

Five locked traits:

1. **Competent** — expertise shown through specificity, not boasts.
2. **Assured** — confident without shouting.
3. **Practical** — load, backup, hours, budget, maintenance, expansion, property type, equipment.
4. **Human** — consequences of unreliable power are treated seriously, without scare tactics.
5. **Responsible** — accountable before, during and after the power comes on.

If copy or visuals fail one of these, they fail the brand.

## 7. Voice & Tone

**Voice (constant):**

> Clear. Calm. Technical. Human. Direct. Assured.

**Model:**

> An experienced engineer explaining an important decision to a smart customer.

**Not:** salesperson, corporate PR, startup marketer, environmental campaign, government agency, reseller, exaggerated luxury.

### Situation matrix

| Situation | Tone |
|-----------|------|
| Hero | Bold, concise, assured |
| Services | Clear, practical, technical |
| Technical explanations | Precise, educational |
| Customer problem | Empathetic, direct — not fearful |
| Process | Calm, structured |
| Proof | Factual, restrained |
| CTA | Welcoming, confident |
| Maintenance | Responsible, reassuring |
| Brand statement | Strong, memorable |
| Legal / technical | Exact, unembellished |

### Voice rules (locked)

1. **Specific over generic.** Prefer load, operating pattern, and system design over “innovative tailored solutions.”
2. **Explain, don’t overwhelm.** Keep technical terms when they help; translate them.
3. **Evidence over adjectives.** Ban unsupported: world-class, revolutionary, unmatched, number one, best-in-class, cutting-edge, game-changing, ultimate.
4. **No fear-based marketing.** Do not manufacture urgency from “terrible electricity.”
5. **No environmental clichés.** Avoid save the planet, greener future, clean energy for a better tomorrow, power the future — unless a later, specific, approved purpose exists.

**Voice test:** Would an experienced installer say this to a smart customer without embarrassment? If not, rewrite.

## 8. Messaging Hierarchy

The page communicates in this order. Product names are not Level 1.

| Level | Focus | Visitor takeaway |
|-------|--------|------------------|
| 1 | **Outcome** | Dependable power |
| 2 | **Approach** | Designed around what actually matters to the property or operation |
| 3 | **Capability** | Assessment, sizing, design, installation, electrical integration, commissioning, support |
| 4 | **Solutions** | Solar, inverter, battery, backup, hybrid, electrical |
| 5 | **Application** | Homes, commercial, estates, healthcare, fuel stations, offices, retail, hospitality, industrial |
| 6 | **Accountability** | Proper installation, testing, commissioning, handover, after-sales |

This prevents a product catalogue and a solar-only homepage.

### Customer questions the page must answer (in order)

1. What does Takeshi do? — Energy systems and electrical infrastructure.
2. Why does it matter? — Unreliable electricity disrupts homes, businesses and property operations.
3. Why this approach? — Design around the actual requirement, not equipment-first selling.
4. Can they handle my property type? — Environments listed above, made immediately scannable.
5. Do they have technical capability? — Named practices (load calculation, sizing, wiring, matching, troubleshooting, commissioning, support).
6. What happens if I engage them? — Clear process.
7. What should I do next? — Start a conversation about energy requirements.

## 9. Core Message Territories

Use as **directions**, not a slogan wall. Repeat the *idea*, not the same sentence.

| Territory | Direction |
|-----------|-----------|
| A — Understand the requirement | We start with the load, not the equipment. |
| B — Design for real use | The right system is the one designed for the way you actually use power. |
| C — Technical integration | Good equipment still needs good system design. |
| D — Professional execution | Installation is only part of the job. Reliable performance requires proper commissioning and ongoing support. |
| E — Specificity | Your power requirements are specific. Your system should be too. |

**Differentiation (locked):** category words such as reliable, affordable, sustainable, quality, professional, experienced are **table stakes**. Takeshi differentiates through the **combination**:

> Understand the actual requirement → design the right system → integrate it properly → commission it → remain accountable.

This describes Takeshi’s supplied approach. It is **not** a claim that competitors never do similar work.

## 10. Colour System

**No additional brand colours** without explicit authorization.

| Hex | Name | Role |
|-----|------|------|
| `#0B0C0A` | Primary authority | Dark field: hero, navigation, major statements, footer, high-impact areas |
| `#F4F5EF` | Primary breathing space | Page background, reading, content, technical explanation |
| `#C6F000` | Energy / action / signal | Primary CTA, active states, highlights, numbers, lines, small accents — **not a page fill** |
| `#3A3E36` | Technical secondary | Dark cards/panels, supporting UI, borders, secondary structure; secondary text **only where contrast permits** |

**Metaphor (locked):** controlled energy.

**Rhythm:** dark structured field → lime signal → warm neutral space.

| Colour | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| Near-black | Authority |
| Lime | Energy / action (electricity moving through a disciplined system) |
| Off-white | Accessibility / human reading space |
| Muted green | Technical grounding |

**Lime constraint (anti-neon):** lime is a **signal**, used sparingly. If a screen looks like a lime solar landing page, the system has failed. Pair lime primarily with `#0B0C0A`. Do not spray lime backgrounds, lime hero fills, or lime-on-pale-lime.

**Accessible premium (locked):** premium does **not** mean low contrast. Premium comes from composition, type, photography, spacing, precision, restraint, hierarchy — not tiny, pale, thin, or sparse type.

Phase 5 implements tokens. Phase 1 only locks roles.

## 11. Typography Direction

**Job of type:** technical authority + editorial clarity + accessibility.

**Direction (not a font pick in code):**

- Strong modern sans-serif
- Highly legible body
- Restrained display
- Clear hierarchy
- Generous but controlled spacing
- Confident treatment of numbers/technical figures where used
- No decorative, futuristic, or “green-tech startup” type
- Contemporary, not trendy

Zenith’s Inter + Satoshi are **scaffold defaults**, not locked Takeshi type. Final families are chosen in Phase 5 against this direction and licensing.

Body size, line length, and contrast must remain readable on mobile. Do not use thin weights as a luxury cue.

## 12. Visual Language

**Concept (locked):** Controlled energy.

**Feel:** structured, editorial, technical, restrained, tactile, human, infrastructure-led, high contrast, purposeful.

**Avoid:** green gradients everywhere; generic solar illustrations; cartoon icons; glassmorphism; futuristic AI aesthetics; floating energy waves; excess glow; neon-tech clichés; generic eco imagery; decorative visual noise; Zenith leftover “solar kit” look.

**Premium definition (locked):**

> Premium = confidence, precision, restraint and quality of communication.

Not expensive-looking. The site should feel like an established technical company that has nothing to prove.

## 13. Photography / Image Strategy

**Feel:** documentary / editorial, not advertising stock.

**Priority:**

1. **Infrastructure** — inverters, battery rooms, panels, distribution, cable management, solar installs, commissioning, electrical work.
2. **Properties** — African/Nigerian homes, commercial buildings, offices, estates, healthcare, fuel stations, retail, hospitality, industrial.
3. **People working** — checking, measuring, installing, testing, inspecting, configuring. Prefer work over posing.
4. **Details** — terminals, breakers, cables, batteries, inverter screens, tools, mounts, hands, indicators. Use as punctuation, not wallpaper.

**Each image must have a job.** If it only fills space, it does not belong.

### Image credibility (locked)

- No image implies a Takeshi project unless verified as one.
- Until project photography exists, sourced images are **illustrative**, labelled or framed as such — never “one of our installations.”
- No fake galleries, fabricated captions, or AI imagery pretending to be project evidence.
- Zenith stock (solar farms, studio headshots, US-coded workers) is **not** Takeshi proof. Phase 4 replaces or honestly reframes it.

## 14. Proof & Credibility Rules

Separate two classes of content:

| Class | What it is | Allowed |
|-------|------------|---------|
| **Verified proof** | Named projects, real photos, real testimonials, certifications, registration, measured results | Only if supplied and approved |
| **Positioning / capability** | What Takeshi does and how it works | Yes, as capability — **not** as statistics |

**Do not invent:** customer counts, MW, kWh, savings, project counts, ratings, awards, certifications, warranties, testimonials, client logos.

**Zenith demo content** (stats, 13M customers, 4.88 rating, NYC portfolio, Springfield address, fictional team, lorem FAQ, USD calculator, US tax credits) is **forbidden as Takeshi evidence**. Phase 0 identified this risk; Phase 2–3 must remove or replace it.

**“10+ years”:** may be used only as supplied experience, accurately worded, and approved in Phase 3. Do not inflate it into a dashboard of fake metrics.

**Brands (Deye, Growatt, etc.):** support technical credibility. They are not the homepage proposition. Message: the right component is the one that works with the system.

**Analytics:** `G-CN09X6ELXM` is a template identifier. It must **not** automatically carry into the final site. Analytics, if any, is an intentional later decision.

**Form:** current contact form is not production-ready (`send_email.js` missing). Do not present it as a working Takeshi channel until a later phase defines a real pathway.

## 15. CTA Strategy

**Primary intent (locked):** start a technical conversation.

**Primary direction:** Discuss your energy needs *(or approved equivalent of that intent).*  
**Secondary direction:** Explore our solutions.

**Journey:** Understand → Trust → Consider → Start a conversation.

**The CTA answers:** “What should I do if I want Takeshi to understand my power requirement?”

**Avoid as primary:** Buy Now, Get the Best Price, Claim Your Quote Today, Act Now, Limited Offer, generic Learn More.

**Final-section intent (copy refined in Phase 3):** Let’s understand what your property actually needs / Tell us about your energy requirements.

**Channel:** one pathway that works for residential and commercial/institutional without a heavy first form. Exact phone, WhatsApp, email: **HUMAN INPUT REQUIRED** — do not invent. Until those exist, strategy still specifies *conversation*, not a fake quote engine.

**Emotional register:** welcoming and confident, not aggressive conversion.

## 16. Information Architecture

**Rule:** the page is a **decision journey**, not a Zenith section collection.

Every section must justify itself against:

1. What is this?  
2. Why does it matter?  
3. Why this approach?  
4. Can they handle my situation?  
5. Do they understand the technical requirements?  
6. What happens next?  
7. How do I start?

If a section cannot, Phase 2 should challenge it.

### Locked one-page story (strategic, not HTML)

| # | Block | Purpose | Answers |
|---|--------|---------|---------|
| 01 | **Hero** | Immediate positioning; promise; next step | What / outcome / approach / next |
| 02 | **Problem / context** | Why dependable power matters for homes, businesses, properties, operations — without fear | Why it matters |
| 03 | **Solutions / services** | Seven supplied services as a system, not a random card grid | What they do |
| 04 | **How Takeshi thinks** | Load → design → match → install → test/commission → support | Why this approach |
| 05 | **Who we serve** | Residential; commercial; estates; healthcare; fuel stations; offices & retail; hospitality; industrial | Can they handle my environment? |
| 06 | **Technical capability** | Named practices; optional approved 10+ years — not a stats wall | Do they understand the work? |
| 07 | **Products / brands** | Experience with named brands + “right component for the system” | Supporting credibility |
| 08 | **How we work** | Understand → Assess → Design → Install → Support (detail: conversation through handover) | What happens next |
| 09 | **Accountability** | Reputation is not built by selling the most equipment; design right, install properly, remain accountable after the power comes on | Trust / difference |
| 10 | **Final CTA** | Informed enquiry | How do I start |

**Services (supplied list, all in scope):**

1. Energy Assessment & System Design  
2. Solar-Power Solutions  
3. Inverter & Battery Systems  
4. Electrical Installation  
5. Hybrid & Backup-Power Systems  
6. Estate & Multi-Unit Installations  
7. Maintenance & After-Sales Support  

**Strategic grouping (labels may be refined in Phase 2):**

> ASSESS → GENERATE → STORE → INTEGRATE → PROTECT → SCALE → MAINTAIN

This is a thinking tool for grouping, not mandatory on-page chrome.

**Nav (strategic):** should follow the journey (e.g. solutions, who we serve, how we work, contact) — **not** Zenith’s Portfolio / Pricing / Blog / Team / Dropdown demo. Exact labels: Phase 2–3.

**Explicitly not required by this IA:** SaaS pricing tiers, fake blog, fake team, fake testimonials, solar savings calculator, nested dropdown demo, auth/coming-soon footer. Phase 2 maps keep/remove/rebuild.

**Content density:** do not paste the full company profile. Use concise sections, meaningful headings, short explanation, technical detail where useful, hierarchy, process, CTAs. Clarity through structure, not omission of substance.

## 17. UX Principles

1. Clarity before decoration.  
2. Evidence before adjectives.  
3. Technical credibility without technical intimidation.  
4. One clear customer journey.  
5. Mobile is a first-class experience.  
6. Accessibility is part of premium quality.  
7. Real infrastructure over generic energy imagery.  
8. Specificity over generic marketing language.  
9. No fabricated proof.  
10. Every visual and section must have a job.

## 18. Accessibility Principles

Accessibility is part of the brand, not a Phase 7 afterthought.

Later implementation must preserve:

- Strong contrast (palette supports this if lime/black/off-white are used as specified)
- Readable type
- Visible focus states (Zenith currently weakens outlines — **document only; do not fix in Phase 1**)
- Keyboard access (Zenith hover-only dropdowns conflict — later)
- Meaningful link/button names
- Semantic heading/landmark hierarchy (`<main>` currently closes early — later)
- Useful alt text; decorative images handled as decorative
- `prefers-reduced-motion`
- Adequate touch targets
- Clear form labels and understandable errors
- Skip link (absent today)

Do not use visual minimalism to reduce usability.

The one-page journey, CTA, services, and contact must work as first-class mobile experiences. Hero 90vh / overlay patterns from Zenith are **suspect** until Phase 6 verifies them. Phase 0 had no live viewport pass; Phase 6 and QA **must** compensate.
