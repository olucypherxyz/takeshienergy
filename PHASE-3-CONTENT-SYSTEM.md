## C. Content transformation principles

1. Substance over completeness — not every true sentence belongs on the page.  
2. One job per section — the story advances; it does not restated.  
3. Scan first — nav, H1, headings, service names, process labels, CTAs must carry the proposition.  
4. Technical terms stay when they add precision; surrounding language carries the property owner.  
5. Capability ≠ proof — describe what Takeshi does; do not imply projects, reach, or results.  
6. Solar is one technology inside a system — never the identity.  
7. CTA vocabulary is small and consistent.  
8. Write for a narrow screen first.

### 03 — SOLUTIONS (+ products)

**Status:** LOCKED  
**ID:** `#solutions`  

**Eyebrow:** What we provide  

**Heading:** Power systems, specified as a whole.  

**Primary idea:**  
Equipment only performs if it is sized, matched and installed as one system. Takeshi specifies around load, backup duration, site conditions and budget.

**Conceptual labels ASSESS → GENERATE → STORE → INTEGRATE → PROTECT → SCALE → MAINTAIN:**  
**Decision: omit as public headings.** They compete with the process section and read like a framework overlay. They **do** govern **order** of the seven services below.

#### Services (order locked)

**1. Energy assessment and system design**  
Site inspection and load evaluation before equipment is recommended. We account for essential appliances and machinery, operating hours, peak demand, backup duration, expansion, site conditions and budget. That determines inverter capacity, storage, generation, protection and supporting electrical work.

**2. Solar-power solutions**  
Supply and installation for homes, offices, commercial sites and institutions — from essential-load backup to hybrid systems that reduce grid and generator use. Panels, inverters, controllers and protection are matched as one design.

**3. Inverter and battery systems**  
New systems, battery replacement, capacity upgrades, fault diagnosis and performance improvement. Capacity follows expected load and required backup time — not a standard kit.

**4. Electrical installation**  
Residential and commercial wiring: new buildings, upgrades, and the work solar and backup systems need. Coordinating this during construction reduces rewiring, improves safety and prepares the property for current or later power systems.

**5. Hybrid and backup-power systems**  
For continuity beyond a simple backup. Configurations may combine grid, solar, batteries, inverters and generators, based on which loads are critical and how much interruption the operation can tolerate.

**6. Estate and multi-unit installations**  
For developers, housing schemes and estate managers: development-wide assessment, standard system options, bulk procurement, phased installation and structured maintenance across units.

**7. Maintenance and after-sales support**  
Inspections, panel cleaning, battery-health checks, connection tightening, wiring review, inverter diagnostics, performance testing and fault resolution. Plans follow the equipment, the site, how hard it is used, and what failure would cost.

#### Products close (Target 07)

**Heading (supporting, not a catalogue title):** Specified for the system, not the showroom.  

**Copy:**  
We have experience supplying and installing equipment from Deye, Growatt, Felicity, Choice and Sukhig, and other certified products when they fit the design and are available. Compatibility, performance, safety and supportability decide the selection. A brand name is not a substitute for correct design.

**Do not imply:** dealership, exclusive partnership, “best inverter,” rankings.

**No CTA in the seven cards.** Journey continues to method, then optional mid-CTA.

### 04 — HOW TAKESHI THINKS (+ capability)

**Status:** LOCKED  
**ID:** `#approach`  

**Eyebrow:** How we think  

**Heading:** We start with the load, not the equipment.  

**Primary idea:**  
The right system is the one designed for how you actually use power. Good equipment still needs that design, plus correct installation, commissioning and support.

**Method (public sequence):**

1. **Understand the load** — what must run, for how long, and at what peak.  
2. **Design the system** — capacity, storage, generation and protection around that requirement.  
3. **Match components** — so parts work as one system, not a pile of products.  
4. **Install correctly** — electrical work included, not treated as an afterthought.  
5. **Test and commission** — prove the system against the intended loads before handover.  
6. **Support** — monitoring, maintenance and fault resolution after the power comes on.

#### Capability close (Target 06)

**Line:** More than ten years of hands-on industry work across:

load calculation · system sizing · installation · electrical wiring · component matching · troubleshooting · commissioning · after-sales support

**Not a second essay. Not a counter widget. Experience stated once.**

### 08 — HOW WE WORK

**Status:** LOCKED  
**ID:** `#process`  

**Heading:** From first conversation to ongoing support.  

**Primary idea:**  
You always know what happens next. The public sequence is five stages. The operational detail sits underneath — not as a second numbered list.

**Stages:**

1. **Understand** — the property, the present supply problems, and the outcome you need.  
2. **Assess** — site and load when the requirement is not already clear.  
3. **Design** — a recommendation: capacity, installation scope, commercial terms, exclusions, maintenance options.  
4. **Install** — procurement, installation, testing and commissioning.  
5. **Support** — handover on safe use and limits, then warranty, service plan or maintenance as agreed.

**Supporting sentence (embeds the long chain without duplicating numbers):**  
Where useful, assessment is followed by a written recommendation, then procurement, installation, testing, commissioning, handover and support.
